# Staying in St Andrews

If you are visiting St Andrews, whether for a short research visit, a conference, or a longer-term stay, organising travel, accommodation, and day-to-day logistics can be confusing. This page brings together useful resources and advice to help make your arrival and stay as straightforward as possible.

If you have any questions, feel free to ask — members of the group are always happy to help with advice on travel, accommodation, and settling into St Andrews.

# Traveling to St Andrews

Information on getting to St Andrews, including options via Edinburgh, Dundee, and other transport links.

**Bus Travel:** The regional bus provider is [**Stage Coach**](https://www.stagecoachbus.com/). They offer a number of useful service and are generally reliable. They have an [app](https://www.stagecoachbus.com/promos-and-offers/national/stagecoachbusapp) that makes purchancing tickets and figuring out routes easy. Of particular note:

-[787 airport service](https://www.stagecoachbus.com/promos-and-offers/east-scotland/jet-747-edinburgh-airport-bus) runs directly from Edinburgh Airport to St Andrews Bus Station every two hours.
-[x59 service](https://www.stagecoachbus.com/routes/east-scotland/x59/edinburgh-bus-station-halbeath-p-r/xhax059.o) runs from the Edinburgh bus station to the St Andrews bus station.
-[x24](https://www.stagecoachbus.com/routes/east-scotland/X24/glasgow-buchanan-bus-station-kincardine-high-street/XHAX024.O) runs from Glasgow Buchanan bus station to St Andrews bus station.
-[99](https://www.stagecoachbus.com/routes/east-scotland/99/st-andrews-bus-station-dundee-city-centre/xhao099.o) runs from Dundee city center to St Andrews with stops in Guard Bridge and at the Luchars Train station (our closest rail connection).

**Train Travel:** St Andrews does not have a train station. The closest railway stations are **Leuchars** (approximately 10 minutes by bus ([99 bus service](https://www.stagecoachbus.com/routes/east-scotland/99/st-andrews-bus-station-dundee-city-centre/xhao099.o)) or taxi from St Andrews) and **Dundee** (approximately 30 minutes by bus).

Train travel is generally the easiest option for longer journeys within Scotland and the UK. Trains from Edinburgh, Glasgow, and other major cities connect through either Leuchars or Dundee.

Useful resources:

- [**Train Line**](https://www.thetrainline.com/information/apps) useful app for booking trains.
- [**ScotRail**](https://www.scotrail.co.uk/) is the main train operator in Scotland. Their website and app are useful for checking routes, timetables, and purchasing tickets.

Of particular note:

- **Edinburgh → Leuchars:** Trains run regularly from Edinburgh Waverley to Leuchars, typically taking around 1 hour. From Leuchars, the **99 bus service** connects directly to St Andrews.
- **Edinburgh → Dundee:** Trains also run regularly to Dundee, with onward buses to St Andrews.
- **Glasgow → Leuchars/Dundee:** Trains generally require a connection (often via Edinburgh or Perth), followed by onward bus travel.
- **London → St Andrews:** The usual route is to take a train from London King's Cross to Edinburgh Waverley, then continue by train to Leuchars and transfer to the bus.
- **Edinburgh Airport → St Andrews:** A tram connection to Ediburgh Gateway/Haymarket gets you on the Edinburgh to Leuchars line.

# Arriving at the Department

Most members of the ODyn group are based in the [**Bute Medical Building**](https://maps.app.goo.gl/YeM8iexrNzzDLLv16) (What3Words: *answer.shirt.hockey*).

Access to the building, and between many areas within it, is controlled by swipe card. If this is your first visit, it is usually easiest to arrange for someone from the group (for example, Graeme or another group member) to meet you at the entrance and let you in.

The Bute Building has several entrances, but the **main entrance adjacent to the car park** is the usual meeting point for visitors. If you are unsure where to go, this is generally the best place to wait.

Visitors who require temporary building access can obtain a visitor access card from the **SEES Front Office** (Room **A??**), which is typically open **09:30–16:30** on weekdays.

## Finding Your Way Around

The Bute Building can be a little confusing to navigate on your first visit. Fortunately, floor plans are displayed throughout the building, and staff and students are always happy to point you in the right direction.

A useful landmark is **the colourful couches**, located on the **A Floor (ground floor)**. From the main entrance, walk to the end of the foyer, turn left through the double doors, and continue up the short ramp. The colourful couches form an informal social hub within the department and are a convenient place to meet before seminars, meetings, or coffee.

## Finding the ODyn Group

Graeme's office is located just beyond the colourful couches in room **A??**, in a small alcove off the main corridor.

Other members of the group are primarily based in two PhD offices on the **B Floor** and a postdoctoral office on the **D Floor**. If you're visiting for the first time, it's usually easiest to ask a member of the group to show you around rather than trying to navigate directly to the offices.
